# Content Sections and Editorial Boundaries

The section names below define intended editorial roles. Where the inspected repository does not prove that a section is implemented on the website, treat it as an editorial series or proposed feature—not as a live website capability.

## 1. Psychology in 5 Minutes

- **Promise:** One concept explained quickly and accurately.
- **Audience need:** Understand a psychological term, mechanism or misconception without reading a long article.
- **Format:** One focused explainer, plain-language definition, one everyday example, 2–4 takeaways and a short evidence note when useful.
- **Topic boundary:** A single concept or question. Avoid attempting to cover a whole condition, long treatment guide or broad multi-part theme in one post.
- **Distinct outcome:** Reader can explain the concept in their own words.
- **Overlap rule:** If a topic already has a five-minute explainer, a longer article must add a different reader question, deeper evidence or practical outcome.

## 2. Psychology Every Day

- **Promise:** A small, relatable psychology insight readers can apply to everyday situations.
- **Audience need:** Recognise patterns in ordinary thoughts, emotions, habits and interactions.
- **Format:** Short weekly post; relatable hook; one insight; one reflection prompt or practical action.
- **Topic boundary:** Everyday applications, not a compressed clinical textbook or an extended treatment article.
- **Distinct outcome:** Reader notices a pattern or tries one small action.
- **Overlap rule:** Avoid reusing the same scenario, lesson and takeaway across consecutive posts. The planned monthly topic list must be imported and reconciled before new topics are assigned.

## 3. Practical Psychology Blogs

- **Promise:** A deeper, evidence-informed guide to a clearly defined concern.
- **Audience need:** Understand a pattern and consider realistic strategies or support options.
- **Format:** Relatable opening; psychological explanation; everyday examples; evidence and limitations; practical strategies; when to seek support; sources.
- **Topic boundary:** A specific problem or outcome, not a catch-all article. Do not diagnose readers or promise guaranteed outcomes.
- **Distinct outcome:** Reader gains a more complete understanding and a set of bounded next steps.
- **Overlap rule:** A blog may expand a short post only if the core question, evidence depth and practical outcomes materially differ.

## 4. The Psychology Compass — Weekly Newsletter (proposed)

- **Promise:** One carefully researched psychology feature each week.
- **Audience need:** A coherent, deeper learning experience with a useful takeaway.
- **Format:** Issue number and date when confirmed; article; key insight; practical reflection; sources; link to the canonical website article when available; platform-specific versions.
- **Topic boundary:** One central question per issue. Avoid repackaging an existing blog without a distinct editorial purpose. A newsletter issue can feature or adapt a blog only if that relationship is explicitly recorded.
- **Distinct outcome:** Reader leaves with a deeper understanding of one question.
- **Implementation status:** Proposed. Native Facebook publishing, website archive, email subscriptions and automated distribution are separate capabilities and must not be described as implemented without verification.

## 5. Topic proposals, awareness campaigns and future series

Campaigns such as mental-health awareness or listening journeys may have a time-bound purpose. Record the campaign and each individual topic separately. Do not treat a campaign title as proof that all planned posts are published. Add a new section to this file before launching a new series, with its audience, promise, format, topic boundaries and overlap rules.

## Cross-section assignment rules

- Assign one **primary section** to each master topic.
- Record secondary appearances as **variants or repurposing**, not duplicate master topics.
- A single source article can have Facebook, WhatsApp and Telegram variants with different lengths and hooks.
- If a topic is reused, record the source topic ID and explain what changes for the new audience or purpose.
- If the new version does not materially change the core question or reader outcome, update the existing item instead of creating a new topic.
